The WanGP view that froze while WanGP kept working, and the parked panel
=======================================================================

2026-09-25. Reported three times over two days as "the wangp frame just
froze": the WanGP page inside the WanGP tab stopped updating and stopped
taking presses - the Advanced toggle did nothing, no progress, no new results
- while jobs sent from the Clipboard tab kept being generated on the server,
and a reload of the frame brought back a page that showed every one of them.
WanGP itself was never stuck. Standalone WanGP, in a browser tab of its own,
has never done this.


1. What the log said
--------------------

The freeze that was caught in a log (logs/wangp-log.txt, 15:00 on the 25th)
rules out the layers this project usually suspects first:

  - The frame had been loaded at 11:35 and never reloaded; no PROXY_STREAM
    line, no heartbeat miss, no lifecycle line but two short trips to the
    background, hours earlier.
  - From 15:00:04 to 15:00:47 the user's presses inside the frame each
    produced a save (a trusted click is a form touch, and a touch is saved a
    second later), and every one of those saves went the whole way: through
    the proxy, into WanGP's Gradio queue, the plugin answered, and the answer
    came back into the page in 34-122 ms. Twelve round trips through the very
    page that was "frozen", all fine.

So the server, the proxy, Forge's connection and the WanGP page's own Gradio
queue were all working at the moment the user's presses did nothing. Whatever
was wrong was inside that page's own behaviour, and the heartbeat could not
see it: the page answered, it just was not WanGP any more.


2. What is different about a frame in a hidden tab
--------------------------------------------------

Two things happen to the WanGP page in this extension that never happen to
standalone WanGP, and both come from being inside a Forge tab:

  - Gradio switches an unselected tab's panel off with an inline
    `display: none` (Gradio 4.40's TabItem sets the style directly; Forge's
    own ui.js reads that attribute to find the current tab). A document
    inside a box that does not exist is not being rendered. Firefox - the
    host's LibreWolf - gives such a document no animation frames at all;
    this project measured that in August, when the bridge's own requests
    queued behind Gradio's frame-scheduled flush until the tab was opened,
    and answered it with the frame timer in the page head. Gradio 5, which
    the WanGP page runs, dispatches its events and applies its component
    updates inside animation frames. The frame timer covers the bridge's
    requests; it was never meant to keep all of WanGP moving.
  - Every return to the tab is a `display: none -> block` transition: a
    relayout of the whole WanGP page, every ResizeObserver in it firing, from
    a state the page has been suspended in for as long as the user was
    elsewhere. The user's pattern is exactly that - hours in the Clipboard
    tab sending jobs, then back to WanGP.

Neither can be proved from the log to be *the* cause: a page that is not
rendered writes no journal. What can be said is that they are the only
differences from the environment where the freeze never happens, and that
the user's own reading of it - "it is running in a frame within a tab that
is often in the background" - names them.


3. What a browser offers, and what it does not
----------------------------------------------

Nothing in a page can raise a frame's priority. The browser renders
documents that have layout boxes, in the tab that is in front, and that is
all. So the choices were: give WanGP a tab of its own (a top-level document
gets everything standalone gets, at the cost of the embedded experience),
give it its own origin and port (the same, plus a changed security model),
or stop hiding the frame. The user wanted the embedded experience kept, so
the frame stopped being hidden.


4. The parked panel
-------------------

The WanGP panel is never `display: none`. `style.css`:

    #tab_wangp[style*="display: none"],
    .minipaint-wangp-tab[style*="display: none"] {
        display: block !important;
        position: fixed; top: 0; left: 0;
        width: var(--minipaint-wangp-parked-width, 100vw);
        overflow: hidden;
        visibility: hidden;
        pointer-events: none;
        z-index: -1;
    }

Keyed on the very inline style Gradio writes, so the panel is parked from its
first paint, with no script in the loop and no flash of it in the page flow;
a page whose bundle never loaded is parked all the same. While its tab is not
the one selected the panel is a rendered box, fixed in the viewport,
invisible, unfocusable (a hidden box is not focusable and not in the
accessibility tree), untouchable, behind everything. Its document keeps its
layout and its animation frames. When the tab is selected, Gradio writes
`display: block`, the rule stops matching, and the panel is back in its
place with nothing to relayout: the bundle kept its width. Whether its
document keeps its animation frames while parked turned out to depend on
the browser - see section 6.

Why `visibility: hidden` and not `opacity: 0`: the assistant in
SD-Neo-ModelSwitchRefiner decides which workspace is on screen by asking
whether a panel's computed visibility is hidden (`visible()` in its
forge_assistant_host.js). A parked WanGP panel answers "not showing" there,
so its focus mode keeps taking the right workspace. An opacity-zero panel
would have read as showing.

Why nothing moves in the DOM: an iframe that is moved reloads its document.
The frame stays where the tab's repair observer, the heartbeat and the
bridge expect it; the only new code is the rule above and a small park
controller in `browser/minipaint_wangp.js`.

What the bundle adds (`watchTabPanel` and friends):

  - It finds the panel from the tab's own root (`closest(".tabitem")`),
    marks it `minipaint-wangp-tab` for a host that named it otherwise, and
    watches its `style` attribute - one element, one attribute.
  - It keeps the panel's width in its place (`--minipaint-wangp-parked-width`,
    measured from the panel while shown and from the tab strip's container
    while parked, so a page that opened on another tab parks WanGP at its
    real width from the start). A fixed box shrink-wraps otherwise, and a
    width that changed with the tab would relayout WanGP on every switch.
  - `fitFrame` fits the frame for where the panel WILL be - under the tab
    strip, plus the column's offset inside the panel - rather than for the
    top of the viewport where it is parked, so the WanGP page is the right
    size before its tab is ever opened; the real measurement corrects any
    difference on the first show.
  - "On screen" for the heartbeat and the leaving flush now means the tab
    is selected AND the frame intersects the viewport. A parked frame does
    the second - it is parked inside the viewport - so intersection alone
    stopped meaning anything.
  - A journal line each way: `tab: the WanGP tab left the screen; its page
    is parked - rendered, invisible, still running` and `tab: the WanGP tab
    is on screen again after Ns parked; meanwhile its page ran N frames by
    the browser and M by the bridge's timer`. M near zero, with N large, is
    the parking working; M large means the page was not being rendered
    after all and the whole diagnosis is wrong.


5. What was measured
--------------------

In headless Chromium, against the real stylesheet (tests/browser_intercept.py,
"the WanGP panel is parked, not hidden"): with txt2img selected the WanGP
panel's inline style says `display: none`, its computed style says block,
fixed, hidden, pointer-events none, and its box is 1216 x N px in a 1400 px
window - the width the bundle kept for it. An iframe under it has a 200 x
100 box; one under a `display: none` box has none. Over one second the
parked iframe received animation frames at the same rate as one under the
tab on screen. Selecting the WanGP tab puts the panel back in flow with the
bundle saying so; leaving parks it again. Taking the `display: block
!important` out of the rule fails exactly the box checks.

What Chromium cannot show is the starvation itself: it ticks frames in a
`display: none` frame too (a probe found 77 frames a second in all three of a
shown, a hidden and a parked frame). Firefox is where a hidden frame gets
none, and the host's browser is Firefox. The journal's frame counts are the
measurement on the machine that matters.

On the virtual clock (tests/test_wangp_protocol.py, `panel_checks`): the
parked page's heartbeat does not beat, a touched form is committed when the
tab is left although the frame never left the viewport, the width is kept,
and five mutations of the controller each fail the check they should.


6. What the machine said
------------------------

Installed on the host (LibreWolf, 2026-09-25 17:54) and reported back the
same evening, the `tab:` lines were unambiguous:

    on screen again after 2008.3s parked; meanwhile its page ran 4 frames
        by the browser and 16119 by the bridge's timer
    on screen again after  598.7s parked; meanwhile its page ran 3 frames
        by the browser and 4854 by the bridge's timer
    on screen again after  424.2s parked; meanwhile its page ran 26 frames
        by the browser and 9305 by the bridge's timer

Four animation frames in thirty-three minutes: Firefox throttles a document
whose embedding element is `visibility: hidden`, which is the very property
section 4 chose, and Chromium - where the suite measures - does not. So the
parked panel does not keep the WanGP page rendering on the browser that
matters. What it still gives is geometry (the page keeps its layout and its
width, a return is not a relayout) and these counts, which are the
measurement that settled the question. `opacity: 0` might escape the
throttle; it would need the sibling assistant's `visible()` taught about
opacity first, and it has not been tried, because the same evening also
showed the freeze does not need a long park at all.

The freeze came back at 23:51 after the tab had been away for seventeen
seconds, and the two logs that arrived with it pointed elsewhere:

  - SD-Neo-ModelSwitchRefiner's llama-server had been placed on WanGP's card
    at 23:45 with 67% of its weights in system RAM ("GPU / VRAM Only could
    not be satisfied - only 21 of 65 layers"), and at 23:50:14 began a reply
    that took 405 s - 325 s reading the prompt at 25 tokens/s, 2.6 tokens/s
    writing - every CPU core for seven minutes, beside a WanGP generation on
    the same card. From that moment the bridge's round trips through the
    WanGP page went from about 60 ms to 500-1600 ms, and stayed there after
    the reload until the reply finished at 23:56:59. A page crawling, not a
    page dead; that placement is the other repository's to fix.
  - The 15:00 freeze had no LLM run and fast round trips, so something else
    is needed for it, and WanGP's Gradio (5.29.0) supplies a candidate that
    fits every observation in both logs: a page's `/heartbeat/<session>`
    stream dropping for any reason marks the session closed (nothing ever
    unmarks it when the browser's EventSource reconnects), and a background
    task deletes a closed session's `gr.State` the moment it is more than
    an hour old - WanGP writes its `state` at page load, so for a page older
    than an hour one blip deletes it at once. Every WanGP handler then gets a
    fresh copy of the build-time state whose `gen` is not the shared
    generation record: queue and progress go blind, handlers fail silently,
    the toggle does nothing, the bridge's own events (which never touch that
    state) keep answering, and a reload is a new session that shows every
    job that ran meanwhile. Embedded, that heartbeat rides Forge's HTTP/2
    connection through Hypercorn and our proxy - the connection that stalled
    on 09-23 after an hour in the background; standalone, it is a localhost
    connection that never blips. The bridge is where that is detected and
    guarded (see the bridge's changelog from 1.8.0 on).


7. What this does not change
----------------------------

The browser tab itself in the background is throttled exactly as standalone
WanGP would be. The proxy and Forge's connection are untouched - the log
says they were not the fault. Forge's own scripts still share the WanGP
page's thread; a heavy gallery update in txt2img can still delay a frame,
which only a tab of its own would end.

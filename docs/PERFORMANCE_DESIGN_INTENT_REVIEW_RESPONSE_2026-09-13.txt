================================================================================
0. VERDICT
================================================================================

Adopt the revised specification. Build from it, not from the original.

The revision is not a stylistic pass over the original. It corrects eight
substantive defects, two of which made parts of the original design
unimplementable as written, and it adds a deployment assumption - a browser on
a different machine from Forge - that changes the transport requirements rather
than merely the acceptance numbers.

Three gaps were found in the revision. All three are clarifications of one or
two sentences, not redesigns. One of them (section 3.1) would produce a
user-visible regression if the specification is implemented exactly as it
currently reads, and should be resolved before Phase 2 begins.

Nothing in this response asks for the revision's architecture to change.

================================================================================
1. VERIFICATION OF THE REVISION'S FACTUAL CLAIMS
================================================================================

The revision makes three concrete assertions about the code that contradict or
resolve open items in the original. All three were checked against the baseline
revision. All three are correct.

1.1 R1 - proxy cache validators are not stripped
------------------------------------------------
CLAIM (revision section 11, R1): response_headers() preserves end-to-end
Cache-Control, ETag and Last-Modified.

VERIFIED CORRECT. minipaint_neo/wangp/proxy.py:367-388.

The function lowercases the upstream pairs, computes `drop` as
hop_by_hop_names(pairs) | {"content-length"}, rewrites `location` through
rewrite_location() and `set-cookie` through rewrite_set_cookie(), and appends
every other header unchanged. hop_by_hop_names() (proxy.py:244-250) returns the
fixed HOP_BY_HOP set plus whatever the connection's own `Connection` header
names. No cache validator appears in either set.

CONSEQUENCE: the original's Q4 was answerable by reading one function and
should not have shipped as an open question. The revision's redirection of the
investigation upstream - does WanGP emit useful validators at all - is the
correct remaining question.

1.2 PAGE_ACTIVE_SECONDS
-----------------------
CLAIM (revision sections B1.3 and 6.13): currently 15 s.

VERIFIED CORRECT. minipaint_neo/clipboard/outbox.py:103, PAGE_ACTIVE_SECONDS =
15.0. Used at outbox.py:734 to decide whether the owner of the head job is
still active. LEASE_SECONDS = 90.0 at outbox.py:100 for comparison.

1.3 enhance.py exposes no completion notification
--------------------------------------------------
CLAIM (revision section B6): the repository does not establish that a
callback/future notification exists; status() is the stable interaction.

VERIFIED CORRECT. minipaint_neo/clipboard/enhance.py public surface is
submit() at :524, status(llm_id) at :583, cancel() at :616, cancel_all() at
:628, plus configuration and capability helpers. There is no callback, future,
condition, subscription or equivalent anywhere in the module.

Whether the external ModelSwitchRefiner API (mc_llm_api) exposes one is
genuinely not determinable from this repository, which is precisely why the
revision's Option A / Option B split is the correct treatment.

CONSEQUENCE: the original's B6 instruction to "await the completion - a
callback, a future, or a condition variable signalled by the enhance module"
asserted a seam that does not exist.

================================================================================
2. CORRECTIONS ACCEPTED
================================================================================

Recorded so that these decisions are not re-litigated later by a reader who
finds the original document. Each entry states what the original said, why it
was wrong, and what the revision does instead.

2.1 The held claim contradicted the one-connection rule
--------------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL: section 6.5 specified a ~25 s held POST claim. Section 2.4 required
exactly one held connection per browser page.

DEFECT: these are mutually exclusive. Once the shared event transport is open,
a held claim is a second long-lived HTTP/1.1 connection against a six-per-origin
budget that Forge also needs.

REVISION: claim stays immediate request/response; a targeted advisory
`claim_ready` event wakes the page; the lease is still granted only under the
outbox lock. This is also strictly more compatible - an old client that keeps
polling the claim route is unaffected, because neither its timing nor its
response shape changes.

ACCEPTED without reservation. The revision's approach is better than the
original's on both correctness and compatibility.

2.2 The original heartbeat was invisible to the browser
--------------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL: section 6.2(d) specified an SSE `:keepalive` comment every 25 s.

DEFECT: EventSource does not surface comment frames to JavaScript. A comment
keeps intermediaries from closing an idle connection, but no browser-side
liveness watchdog can be built on it. The original then had no mechanism at all
for detecting a silently black-holed connection.

REVISION: an application-visible `heartbeat` event, carrying no id and not
advancing the state revision, approximately every 15 s, feeding a local
`lastFrameAt` watchdog with a 35-45 s deadline.

ACCEPTED. This is the difference between a design that detects a dead remote
link and one that does not.

2.3 A5 raised latency and violated the original's own principle
----------------------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL: raise webui-host.js wait_until's default interval from 50 ms to
150 ms, on the reasoning that waiting faster than the ~100 ms producer "buys
nothing."

DEFECT: the reasoning is backwards. The two timers are unsynchronised, so mean
detection delay is approximately half the observer's interval: ~25 ms at 50 ms,
~75 ms at 150 ms. The change adds latency. It also violated the original's own
section 2.1, which forbids lengthening an interval as an optimisation.

REVISION: retain the current default unless a measured replacement proves
neutral or faster; prefer an observable host state if Forge exposes one.

ACCEPTED.

2.4 B4 as originally written would not have fired
--------------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL: observe the acknowledgement textarea's `input` event, or attach a
MutationObserver scoped to that element.

DEFECT: programmatic assignment to a textarea's `value` property emits no
`input` event. MutationObserver observes the `value` *attribute* - which is the
element's default value - not the property. Neither mechanism reliably sees the
write the bridge actually performs.

REVISION: prototype three ordered options (a real Gradio component event, a
narrow setter/update seam, MutationObserver only if a prototype proves the
installed update path mutates observed DOM); if none is reliable, keep the
value-read fallback but start it after a ~2 s grace and run it only while a
bridge request is in flight.

ACCEPTED. The revision's section 9.6 test list is the right way to hold this.

2.5 B6 assumed a notification seam that does not exist
-------------------------------------------------------
See 1.3. ACCEPTED. The revision's Option B constraint - a server-side bounded
observer that queries the external API directly and does not re-read and
rewrite the whole outbox document to discover progress - preserves the actual
performance goal without inventing an API.

2.6 B2's justification was wrong
---------------------------------
ORIGINAL: "While a job is merely `waiting`, its position only changes when
something ahead of it finishes - which is a `job` event the server already
publishes. There is nothing to poll for."

DEFECT: WanGP's queue may contain work created directly in WanGP or by another
integration. MiniPaint's own outbox events cannot prove those external queue
movements. The claim that there is nothing to observe was load-bearing for the
original's conclusion and is false.

REVISION: states the correction explicitly and permits an adaptive bounded
cadence under Option 2 as the documented exception to the server-side
preference.

ACCEPTED. This was the most consequential error in the original.

2.7 Q4 was answerable from the code
------------------------------------
See 1.1. ACCEPTED.

2.8 The original assumed loopback deployment
---------------------------------------------
ORIGINAL: section 7 thresholds were stated for loopback, "which is the
deployment target."

DEFECT: that assumption was asserted, not established. If the browser may be on
another machine, then transient disconnects, browser sleep, proxy buffering,
VPN reconnect and server restart are normal conditions. The original had no
`server_epoch`, no authoritative reconcile route, and no answer for a TCP path
that fails without producing an application error.

REVISION: sections 0 (deployment assumption), 1.4, 2.3, 2.9, 6.3, 6.9, 6.11 and
7.4. Normal path is push; recovery is one authoritative sync; recovery is not a
loop.

ACCEPTED, and this is the revision's most valuable addition. The original's
reliability argument rested on "EventSource usually reconnects," which is not a
proof that no stale state survived an interruption. The epoch-namespaced cursor
plus the sync route is that proof.

3. GAPS FOUND IN THE REVISION
================================================================================

Three items. Proposed resolution text is given for each so they can be dropped
in without further design work.

3.1 HIDDEN TABS WILL STALL THEIR OWN QUEUES
--------------------------------------------
Severity: user-visible regression. Resolve before Phase 2.
Affects: revision sections 6.10 (VISIBILITY hidden) and 6.13.

THE CHAIN:

    section 6.10  hidden -> close the transport
    section 6.13  transport disconnect -> 15 s grace -> page marked inactive
                  -> no claim_ready
    consequence   the page stops claiming its own work

WHY NOBODY ELSE PICKS IT UP: a job can only be run by the page that composed
it. In claim() (outbox.py:722-739) a page is handed `head` only when
head["page"] == page_id, and otherwise `mine[0]` - its own oldest waiting job.
Page presence governs only whether *other* pages may skip ahead of an inactive
page's head job; it never transfers ownership. So a hidden page's jobs are not
reassigned. They wait for that page to become visible again.

WHAT HAPPENS TODAY: pump() waits with setTimeout. Chrome throttles background
timers to roughly once per minute after approximately five minutes hidden, but
does not stop them, and fetch responses still resolve. A hidden tab therefore
continues to drain its queue, slowly.

WHAT HAPPENS UNDER THE REVISION AS WRITTEN: it drains at zero.

USER-VISIBLE FORM: queue ten items, switch to another tab for twenty minutes,
return to find two were sent. This will be reported as a bug, and it will be
attributed to the performance work.

AGGRAVATING DETAIL: section 6.10 currently permits "close the transport after a
short grace or immediately if implementation is simpler." The simpler
implementation is the one that causes this.

PROPOSED RESOLUTION - replace the VISIBILITY hidden clause in section 6.10
with:

    VISIBILITY hidden:
        - stop all rendering and layout work immediately
        - if this page has no non-terminal work of its own, close the
          transport and mark continuity uncertain
        - if this page has non-terminal work of its own (pending, enhancing,
          sending, queued or started), KEEP the transport open so the page
          continues to receive claim_ready and continues to drain its own
          queue. A hidden page must not stall work that only it can run.
        - a page that keeps its transport open while hidden performs no
          rendering and no speculative fetching; it claims, runs and reports
          exactly as a visible page does

and add to section 6.13:

    A page that holds non-terminal work of its own remains present while
    hidden. Presence is not a proxy for visibility; it is a statement that the
    page is still able to run the work it owns.

SECONDARY CHECK: confirm that a browser tab which the OS or browser genuinely
freezes (not merely hides) is still recovered by the existing visibility-restore
sync path. Freezing is outside the extension's control; the requirement is only
that recovery is correct, not that the connection survives.

3.2 TARGETED EVENTS IN A SHARED RING ARE UNSPECIFIED
-----------------------------------------------------
Severity: low. Specification gap, not a defect.
Affects: revision sections 6.4, 6.6, 6.8.

Section 6.4 defines a single bounded ring (recommended 512 entries) with
"subscribers keyed by page." Section 6.6 defines `claim_ready` as a hint
targeted at one page. It is not stated whether targeted events enter the ring.

IF THEY DO: replay after a gap delivers page A's claim_ready to page B. This is
harmless in correctness terms - claim_ready is advisory, carries no lease, and
a wasted claim returns empty - but it multiplies useless claim calls by the
number of open tabs on every eligibility change, which is the traffic the work
exists to remove.

IF THEY DO NOT: claim_ready cannot be replayed. This is fine, because B1 item 8
already requires maybeClaim() once after enqueue, reconnect, sync, retry or
adopt, which covers exactly the case replay would have covered.

The second reading is almost certainly what is intended. The risk is that it
gets decided by accident during implementation.

PROPOSED RESOLUTION - add to section 6.4:

    Targeted events (currently claim_ready) are delivered only to their
    addressed subscriber and are NOT placed in the shared replay ring. They are
    recomputed from current eligibility on connect and on sync, which is what
    B1 item 8 already requires. Only broadcast durable-state events enter the
    ring.

3.3 WHETHER claim_ready ADVANCES THE REVISION IS UNDEFINED
-----------------------------------------------------------
Severity: low. One sentence.
Affects: revision section 6.3.

Section 6.3 gives two rules: every durable state event gets the next revision;
heartbeat does not advance revision and carries no id. `claim_ready` is neither
a durable state change nor a heartbeat, and no rule covers it.

IF IT ADVANCES: every page's cursor moves for an event most of them did not
need. Workable, but section 6.10's gap-detection and duplicate-revision logic
must tolerate a cursor advancing with no state the page cares about having
changed.

IF IT DOES NOT: it cannot be replayed - which is consistent with 3.2 and is the
coherent choice.

PROPOSED RESOLUTION - add to section 6.3, alongside the heartbeat rule:

    Targeted advisory events (claim_ready) do not advance the state revision
    and carry no id, for the same reason heartbeat does not: they describe an
    opportunity, not a durable state change, and they are recomputed on connect
    and sync rather than replayed.

This makes 6.3, 6.4 and 6.8 mutually consistent.

4. SCOPE OBSERVATION
================================================================================

Not a defect. Delivery-risk control only, offered for the implementing
engineer's judgement.

The SSE -> long-poll fallback (revision section 6.12) is well-specified and is
justified by the remote deployment assumption. It is also a substantial piece of
work: a second transport, a never-overlap invariant, transition-back-at-
lifecycle-boundary logic, and its own share of the test matrix in section 9.4.

Section 8 builds it in Phase 2, before any evidence from real use.

Prototype P1 in Phase 0.5 already tests SSE on the actual remote topology - the
real LAN, VPN, tunnel or reverse proxy in use. If P1 passes cleanly on those
paths, the fallback could be deferred behind a documented trigger and built when
a deployment demonstrates it is needed.

Deferring costs nothing architecturally: the cursor, epoch, replay, reset and
event taxonomy are already transport-independent by construction, which is
exactly what section 6.12 says.

If the engineer already knows that a proxy in the real path buffers or closes
text/event-stream, disregard this entirely and build it in Phase 2 as written.

5. CHECKED AND FOUND SOUND - NO ACTION
================================================================================

Recorded so that review effort is not spent on these again.

    - Publication as a property of _save (revision 6.5). Verified: all durable
      outbox mutations funnel through _save(jobs) at outbox.py:364, with 18 call
      sites (547, 567, 579, 587, 680, 719, 749, 771, 792, 812, 817, 829, 854,
      887, 927, 951, 970, 990), every one already inside `with _lock:`. Making
      publication a property of saving is sound and cannot be forgotten by a
      nineteenth mutation.

    - Thread-safety boundary (revision 6.4). Correct. Synchronous mutation code
      must not touch another thread's asyncio primitives;
      loop.call_soon_threadsafe or an equivalent bridge is required. The
      instruction not to perform socket writes under the outbox lock is
      correct and important.

    - Privacy treatment (revision 6.6, 9.8). Correct and stricter than the
      original. Event and sync payloads are writers subject to
      test_logging_privacy.py without exception.

    - STREAM_IDLE_TIMEOUT (revision B8). The revision's refusal to make it
      finite merely because an infinite timeout looks suspicious is correct.
      The value is deliberate; changing it needs a proven liveness signal on
      the relevant upstream stream, not an aesthetic judgement.

    - Sync returning all jobs rather than only this page's. Consistent with the
      existing GET /minipaint-interop/outbox route, which already returns
      box.jobs() unfiltered (interop.py:542). No new exposure.

    - Acceptance methodology (revision 7.1). p50/p95/p99 over repeated runs,
      with network RTT reported separately, is the right replacement for the
      original's single mean/worst figures.

    - Migration sequence (revision 8). The Phase 0.5 prototype gate is a genuine
      improvement on the original, which deferred its open questions to a
      section at the end rather than making them block the work that depends on
      them.

    - C1 build tooling (revision, section C1). Correct that minification must
      not smuggle in an unapproved build dependency, and correct that a
      hand-maintained minified artifact with no deterministic regeneration path
      is worse than no minification. Lazy loading is the real fix either way.

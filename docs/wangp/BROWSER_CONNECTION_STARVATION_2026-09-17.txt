MINIPAINT NEO - THE DAY THE BROWSER FROZE
INCIDENT REPORT AND CHANGE RECORD: CONNECTION STARVATION UNDER THE WANGP TAB

Repository target: RJSprod/a1111-mini-paint-NEO
Code baseline:     35c534a (main, 2026-09-17), the merge of PR #87
Logs read:         send-log.txt, wangp-log.txt (the WanGP tab's journal),
                   llama-server.log and model_chain.log from
                   SD-Neo-ModelSwitchRefiner, all of 2026-09-17
Revision date:     2026-09-17
Status:            Incident report, with what was built in answer to it.
                   Section 6 is what is left, and it is structural.

================================================================================
0. VERDICT
================================================================================

What froze was the browser, not the server.

A browser allows six persistent HTTP/1.1 connections to one origin. Every tab
of this WebUI shares that budget with everything else on the same origin, and
by the afternoon of the 17th five of the six were long-lived streams: Forge's
own heartbeat, Forge's queue, this extension's event spine, and the WanGP
iframe's heartbeat and queue, the last two carried through our reverse proxy
on the Forge origin. When the WanGP child stopped answering, the two proxied
streams stayed open on a server that would never speak again - the proxy had
no idle bound on a stream, on purpose - and the sixth connection was a proxied
request waiting on the same server. From that moment every request the page
made queued in the browser behind them: the Clipboard's queue polls, the
text-to-image generation, the "Restart WanGP now" button, this tab's own
journal. The server was fine the whole time. It finished jobs for nine minutes
after the browser's last line, and the one thing that freed the page was
restarting the browser, which is the one thing that drops every connection.

The in-place recovery specification implemented in PR #87 did not fall short
of what it set out to do; it set out to do something else. It answers an
iframe that GOES AWAY while the tab still wants one. On the 17th the iframe
never went anywhere: it stayed in the page, its document alive, holding the
connections. Section 3 has the one assumption of that design this incident
showed to be wrong, and the fix.

================================================================================
1. WHAT HAPPENED
================================================================================

From the WanGP tab's journal and the extension's send log, all local time:

    00:00:31  RecursionError inside a WanGP tab event (the cyclic per-page
    00:03:25  gr.State that Gradio hashes before every call). Again at
    00:04:00  09:18:03 and 12:44:36. Nuisance, not cause: each fails one
              press and nothing else.
    09:16:25  A burst of 24 queue polls fails with NetworkError, then the
              page reloads at 09:16:36. The same shape at 12:40:34/12:40:39
              and 16:57:44/16:57:47: requests aborted by a reload, not a
              server refusing them.
    12:47:55  Forge restarted; WanGP running again at 12:51:19.
    15:29:24  Forge restarted; WanGP running again at 15:31:05.
    16:35:03  The browser's last line. From here nothing this page sends
              reaches the server.
    16:44:04  The server's executor completes its last job of the day. It
              was never stuck.
    ~16:50    "Restart WanGP now" pressed; the panel spins past 130 s. The
              press never left the browser.
    16:57:47  The browser is restarted. WanGP and the WebUI work at once, with
              no restart of either.

Every page load in that journal landed on a WanGP tab painted when Forge was
BUILT: the card, the state box and the iframe are rendered once at build time
and nothing repainted them until a button was pressed. "Start WanGP" over a
WanGP that had been serving for an hour was the ordinary look of the tab
after a reload, and the user pressed Start or Check again after every one.

On the ModelSwitchRefiner side, llama-server ran its model on the CPU with 16
threads - every core of the machine - for nine minutes of the afternoon, and
its GPU placement pointed at GPU 0, the card WanGP owns. That starves WanGP
of CPU while it generates and competes for its memory. It did not cause the
freeze, and it makes WanGP slower to answer, which is how the streams above
come to be silent long enough for any of this to matter.

================================================================================
2. WHY: THE SIX-CONNECTION BUDGET
================================================================================

Firefox and LibreWolf: network.http.max-persistent-connections-per-server,
default 6. Chromium, and so Via on Android: six per host. Both count a
server-sent-events stream as a connection for as long as it is open, because
it is one.

What one loaded page of this WebUI holds on the Forge origin:

    1  /heartbeat/<session>            Forge's page, for its whole life
    2  /queue/data?session_hash=       Forge's page, while anything is queued
    3  /minipaint-interop/events       this extension's event spine
    4  /wan2gp/heartbeat/<session>     the WanGP iframe, through the proxy
    5  /wan2gp/queue/data              the WanGP iframe, through the proxy
    6  everything else, one at a time

"Everything else" is every button press, every file, every poll, every
thumbnail, and every request the WanGP page itself makes. It serialises
through one connection, and any single request that stalls stalls all of it.
The proxy allowed a proxied response 120 seconds and a proxied stream for
ever; a wedged WanGP therefore held connections 4 and 5 for ever and 6 for
two minutes at a time. That is the freeze.

HTTPS through the auto-TLS extension changes nothing here. uvicorn speaks
HTTP/1.1 over TLS; the six-connection rule is per origin and the same either
way. Section 6 says what would change it.

================================================================================
3. WHY THE RECOVERY DESIGN DID NOT COVER IT, AND THE ASSUMPTION IT GOT WRONG
================================================================================

The recovery design watches for the iframe ELEMENT to leave the DOM and, when
the tab still wants one, presses the tab's hidden Refresh so the server paints
another. On the 17th the element never left. A page cannot see a connection
budget; what it can see is the one thing that came for free - the event spine
going silent while the server was, in fact, speaking - and nothing listened
for that. Section 4.3 is that listener.

The assumption: the design, and a comment in the wizard's own code, held that
Gradio re-renders an HTML component it is handed even when the string is the
one it already holds, and that a repaint is therefore always a WanGP page
load. It is not. Gradio 4.40's frontend applies an output through Svelte's
safe_not_equal, and two equal strings are not a change: innerHTML is left
alone, and a missing iframe stays missing. Every paint whose purpose is a new
iframe now stamps it with a paint token (a data attribute the browser never
reads), so the string is new and the element is re-created. The hidden
Refresh, the restart paths and the management panel's paints use it; every
other paint deliberately does not, so an iframe that is showing is left alone.

================================================================================
4. WHAT WAS BUILT
================================================================================

4.1  The proxy (minipaint_neo/wangp/proxy.py)
     STREAM_IDLE_TIMEOUT is 60 seconds where it was None. Gradio's own
     streams are never quiet - /heartbeat pings and /queue/data heartbeats
     every 15 seconds - so a minute of silence is a child whose event loop is
     not running, not a slow generation. Cutting it gives the connection back;
     Gradio's client reconnects on its own.

4.2  The event spine in the browser (browser/minipaint_interop.js)
     The stream's watchdog says what it sees, on the document, as the one
     "minipaint:outbox" event with kind "stream": "silent" after 40 seconds
     with no frame, at which point it sends one plain snapshot request;
     "answered" when that request comes back, however it comes back; "open"
     when the stream is up again. A request that never comes back says
     nothing, which is the whole signal.

4.3  The WanGP tab (browser/minipaint_wangp.js)
     A transport breaker. On "silent" it waits 20 seconds of ON-SCREEN time
     (the same visibility-aware deadline every other budget in the file
     uses) for "answered" or "open". If neither comes, nothing from this page
     is getting a connection, and the tab gives back the two that are its
     own: the iframe is navigated to about:blank, which needs no connection
     and unloads the WanGP document, and with it every stream and request
     that document held. The browser side of the bridge is retired the way a
     lost frame retires it (in-flight mutating requests settle UNCONFIRMED,
     never as refusals). WanGP is loaded again in the same element, at the
     same path, the moment the spine says the transport is back - Forge's
     queued requests get the freed connections first - and after 15 seconds
     on screen regardless, because a blank tab is not a recovery. A page on a
     card has nothing to shed and says so. Nothing binds to the blank frame
     in between.

     A boot repaint. The first time the tab's root is found with no iframe
     under it, the hidden Refresh is pressed once: the card was painted when
     Forge started, and this paints what is true now. It starts nothing.

     Runtime frames. The runtime announces every change of state on the spine
     (4.5). A page whose tab disagrees - WanGP serving under a card, or gone
     under an iframe - presses Refresh, once per burst, judged again at the
     moment of pressing against the latest frame and the tab as it is then.
     The snapshot's runtime summary counts the same way.

4.4  The Clipboard tab (browser/minipaint_clipboard.js)
     One read of the queue route at a time: a second ask while one is out
     gets the same answer. Every ask, read or write, is bounded at 25 seconds
     by an AbortController, and the slot is released however the read ends.
     The day it mattered, 24 reads of this route were in flight at once.

4.5  The runtime (minipaint_neo/wangp/runtime.py)
     Every transition - ready, stopped, crashed, a failed start - is published
     on the event spine as {state, running} and nothing else. The emergency
     restart's escalation, which was a POSIX-only branch that did nothing on
     Windows, now ends each surviving pid its own way (SIGKILL, or
     TerminateProcess through a handle opened for exactly that right); a pid
     that survives even that is reported as stuck in a driver call, which is
     what it is, rather than as this extension's failure. nvidia-smi is not
     asked a second time when it did not answer the first.

4.6  Tests
     wangp protocol 497 (twenty new scenarios on the virtual-clock DOM
     harness: the breaker armed, disarmed, starved, hidden, with no frame,
     never answered; the boot repaint; runtime frames agreeing, disagreeing,
     bursting, and caught up; eight new mutations, each reverting one
     decision and named for the check that must then fail), interop browser
     32 (a stream that goes quiet says "silent" then "answered" and reopens;
     one whose request never returns never says "answered"), wangp runtime
     146 (the spine announcements; the escalation by pid; the survivor's
     wording; _force_exit on a real child and on a pid that is nobody's),
     wangp proxy 181 (the idle bound clears several heartbeats and is still a
     bound), receiver contract 345 (a stamped paint is a different string
     that points at the same path), browser clipboard 244 (three asks over
     one held read open no second connection).

================================================================================
5. THE SAME DAY, REPLAYED
================================================================================

WanGP wedges mid-generation. Its two proxied streams go quiet; at 60 seconds
the proxy cuts them and the browser has two connections back. Any proxied
request still waiting is cut at 120 seconds. If the budget was already spent,
this extension's spine goes quiet too: at 40 seconds the interop layer says
so and sends one snapshot request; if that comes back, the silence was the
stream's alone and nothing more happens; if it does not come back in 20
seconds on screen, the WanGP tab unloads its iframe. The freed connections
carry Forge's queued requests - the generation that was pressed, the Restart
button, the snapshot - and the moment the snapshot answers, WanGP is loaded
again. If the child is still wedged, that load shows the proxy's own error
page inside the tab, and "Restart WanGP now" works, because it is Forge's
request and Forge's connections are free. Nothing in this asks the user to
do anything, and the Canvas, the Clipboard and the queue are untouched.

What is still true: a WanGP process stuck inside the GPU driver cannot be
ended by anything short of the driver letting go, and the restart report now
says exactly that when it happens.

================================================================================
6. THE STRUCTURAL CURE, AND WHAT IS LEFT AFTER IT
================================================================================

The budget is the problem, and only the transport can change the budget.

    a)  HTTP/2 in front of Forge. SHIPPED the same day, in the auto-TLS
        extension (RJSprod/NEO-webui-auto-tls-https, pull requests 2 and 3),
        because that extension already owns the TLS side and browsers speak
        HTTP/2 only over TLS. It wraps Gradio's own ``start_server`` and, when
        Gradio asks for TLS, serves the same ASGI app through Hypercorn with
        ALPN h2 and http/1.1; every request and stream of a page then shares
        ONE connection and no six-stream rule applies. Without TLS, with
        ``--autotls-http1``, or if Hypercorn cannot start, Gradio's own server
        runs and says so.

        Two things that fell out of shipping it, both worth remembering.
        Only Hypercorn's public serve()/Config API survives across releases -
        the first host to run it had the Hypercorn 0.13 that the old certipie
        dependency pinned years ago, which has none of the internals and which
        an installer asking only "is it installed?" never upgraded. And a
        certificate pair the server cannot load has to be caught before a
        socket is bound, because ssl.SSLError is an OSError and a port scan
        reads that as "taken".

        This is the cure; everything in section 4 is the mitigation that keeps
        a page alive when the cure is not in place - an older auto-TLS, a
        launch without TLS at all, or a browser that negotiated http/1.1.

    b)  A second origin for the WanGP proxy - another port on the same host
        - doubles the budget and puts the iframe's streams in their own.
        Cookies are host-scoped so sign-in carries over, but the bridge's
        postMessage origin checks and the proxy's Origin gate would both
        have to learn the second origin. Smaller than (a) in infrastructure,
        larger in this code.

    c)  On the host, LibreWolf's network.http.max-persistent-connections-per-
        server can be raised in about:config. Via on Android cannot be
        changed, which is why (c) is not an answer on its own.

A session that lasts days is a browser that is never starved, plus a page
that can be told what changed rather than asking. The second half is what
the event spine and the runtime announcements are for; the first half is
(a). Until (a), the breaker above is the guarantee: the page frees itself
within about a minute of the budget being spent, without a reload and
without losing what the server holds.

On the ModelSwitchRefiner side: cap llama-server's threads below the core
count when its model runs on the CPU, keep it off the GPU WanGP owns (the
MiniPaint config names that card by UUID under gpu.uuid), and do not run a
CPU model while a video is generating. None of that is this repository's to
change; it is written here because the logs made the interaction visible.
